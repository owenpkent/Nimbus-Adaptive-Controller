## A. The problem, and the vocabulary

**Position control against rate control.** A mouse maps device displacement to pointer displacement. A gamepad stick maps deflection to angular velocity: the camera keeps turning while the stick is held. Nimbus converts the first into the second, which `AIM_ASSISTANCE.md` section 3 already identifies as the structural cost of the architecture. Everything in this dossier about control laws (part D.6) follows from it: a tool that drives a mouse issues a displacement once; a tool that drives a stick runs a velocity servo on an angular error, through a delay.

**What "aimbot" means in practice.** Read the rendered frame or game memory, find enemies, compute the offset from the crosshair, move the input onto the target, often fire. The public tools in part D.4 all share five components: capture, detection, target selection, a control law, and an output path. The commercial vocabulary is stable: "FOV" is the pixel radius around the crosshair inside which a detection may be engaged, "smoothing" is the fraction of the remaining error applied per tick, "prediction" is a velocity lead, "humanization" is added jitter to defeat behavioural detection, "triggerbot" fires when a target crosses the crosshair, "anti-recoil" cancels the game's kick.

**The games-HCI taxonomy** (part E.2) uses different words for milder things: area cursor (a bigger hit region), bullet magnetism (the projectile bends), sticky targets (the cursor slows over a target), target gravity (the cursor is steered toward a target), target lock (snap and hold), slowdown, auto-aim. Console "aim assist" is slowdown plus bounded rotation (`AIM_ASSISTANCE.md` section 7).

## B. First-party and game-native aim assistance, 2023 to 2026

### B.1 Platform holders

**Microsoft.** Gaming Copilot for Xbox Series X|S was announced at the GDC Festival of Gaming in March 2026 (Sonali Yadav, product manager for gaming AI); accessibility use cases were described as being "explored" with no specifics ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-50342), [GamingHQ](https://gaminghq.eu/2026/03/15/microsoft-gaming-copilot-xbox-2026/)). On 2026-05-05 and 06 Xbox CEO Asha Sharma announced Microsoft "will begin winding down Copilot on mobile and will stop development of Copilot on console" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/personal-tech/2026/05/06/its-game-over-for-copilot-on-xbox/5230456), [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/xbox-no-longer-developing-copilot-for-consoles/)). No first-party AI aim assistance was ever specified. The Windows Game Bar Gaming Copilot is the relevant precedent for a first-party tool that reads the game screen: it screenshots gameplay, runs OCR and sends the result to Azure, and drew a privacy backlash in October 2025 for doing so by default ([WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2025/10/26/microsoft-defends-gaming-copilot-privacy-after-backlash-over-hidden-screenshot-data-capturing-xcxwbn/), [DoublePulsar](https://doublepulsar.com/microsoft-builds-on-recall-with-gaming-copilot-fails-basic-privacy-tests-52988576bcc8)). A first-party tool that reads the screen is established; one that reads the screen and steers input is not.

**Xbox Copilot mode**, now called Controller Assist, lets two controllers act as one player on Xbox One, Series and Windows: Settings, Accessibility, Controller, Co-pilot ([SpecialEffect GameAccess](https://gameaccess.info/how-to-set-up-copilot-with-an-xbox-adaptive-controller-on-the-new-xbox/), [TechBabble, 2025-09-21](https://techbabble.co.uk/2025/09/21/how-do-i-use-the-xbox-adaptive-controller-in-co-pilot-mode/)). This is human shared control, the closest platform-holder analogue to software shared control. Hardware in the period: the Xbox Adaptive Joystick, announced August 2024, released March 2025 at 29.99 USD ([Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2024/08/21/xbox-accessibility-adaptive-joystick-thumbstick-toppers/)).

**Sony.** The Access controller launched December 2023 with four 3.5 mm expansion ports, developed with AbleGamers, SpecialEffect and Stack-Up ([SIE](https://sonyinteractive.com/en/press-releases/2023/access-controller-launch/)). Sony's own PS5 accessibility history to 2025 lists no platform-level aiming assist ([SIE blog](https://sonyinteractive.com/en/news/blog/accessibility-and-ps5-our-history-of-features-from-launch-to-2025/)). A "ghost player" patent filed September 2024 describes an AI that takes over the character on request; it is a patent, and the coverage framing it as accessibility is speculation ([Futurism](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/sony-patents-ai-plays-video-games)). Sony also formed a 15-member PlayStation Studios Accessibility Community Council ([SIE](https://sonyinteractive.com/en/news/blog/playstation-studios-accessibility-community-council/)). No 2026 announcement of AI-driven aim assistance from Microsoft, Sony or Nintendo was found; treat as absent, not missed.

**Valve, Steam Input.** A first-party input transformer that games and anti-cheats accept: gyro-to-mouse on PS4, PS5, Switch Pro and Steam Deck pads ([Valve gyro guide](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/steam_hardware/announcements/detail/685257114654870229)); mouse regions mapping an input's range 1:1 onto a screen area ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/steam_controller/mouse_regions)); the guidance that developers expose an `absolute_mouse` action so gyro and trackpad get 1:1 deltas ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/steam_controller/getting_started_for_devs)); per-game anti-deadzone ([controller mapping guide](https://controllertesting.com/learn/steam-controller-mapping-guide)). None is target-aware.

**Tobii, Aim at Gaze.** The only first-party-adjacent target-aware input guideline: snap to a target "when the gazepoint is close to a target, when initiating Aim Mode"; Shadow of the Tomb Raider moves the crosshair, Assassin's Creed Origins moves the camera; framed as both gameplay and accessibility ([Tobii Developer Zone](https://developer.tobii.com/pc-gaming/design-guidelines/explored-features/aim-at-gaze/)).

## C. Anti-cheat and publisher positions, 2023 to 2026

### C.1 Activision, RICOCHET

- **January 2024.** RICOCHET began detecting "keyboard and mouse players activating aim assist through unauthorized tools", naming Cronus Zen, XIM and reWASD; on detection "Ricochet will promptly close down the Call of Duty application", with account action on repeat ([AltChar](https://www.altchar.com/game-news/activisions-ricochet-anti-cheat-takes-a-stand-against-aim-assist-exploits-on-keyboard-and-mouse-in-call-of-duty-asAx57o4I1sl), [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/consoles-pc/call-of-dutys-anti-cheat-update-will-close-down-the-game-if-it-detects-aim-assist-on-mouse-and-keyboard) **[headline and summary only; article body not fetched]**).
- **2026-02-03, Season 02 post.** "These devices are not permitted in Call of Duty. They are cheating tools, even if they masquerade as accessibility devices." Detection is behavioural: "We analyze input timing, consistency, and response patterns to distinguish natural human play from machine-modified input," with a ladder of warning, then cloaking or disarming, then ban. The sentence that "accessibility is important" and that Call of Duty offers "in-game options and supported hardware" is reported by search summaries only. The [official post](https://www.callofduty.com/blog/2026/02/call-of-duty-black-ops-7-ricochet-anti-cheat-season-02) would not load; the quotes are confirmed by [HipHopWired](https://hiphopwired.com/3029460/call-of-duty-ricochet-cronus-xim-cheaters-details/) and [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/call-of-duty/black-ops-7-season-2-anti-cheat-update) **[primary not fetched]**.
- **2026-05-22 and 23, the WheeledGamer case.** A paralysed Dallas streamer on a QuadStick was temp-banned "for using a third-party input modification device". After public pressure Call of Duty replied "Your account has been unbanned" and said #TeamRICOCHET would contact him "to learn more about your setup" ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/twitch/paralyzed-cod-warzone-streamer-begs-activision-for-help-after-accessibility-controller-ban-3367476/), [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/news/31586/paralyzed-call-of-duty-streamer-banned-for-using-accessibility-controller)). No published exemption or whitelisting process exists. QuadStick on console typically runs through a Titan Two, the adapter class RICOCHET targets ([QuadStick group](https://groups.google.com/g/quadstick/c/KhXQgGASICc)).
- **The Security and Enforcement Policy** defines unauthorized software to include "input mapping software" ([Activision SEP](https://support.activision.com/articles/call-of-duty-security-and-enforcement-policy)), which on its face covers any virtual-gamepad remapper, Nimbus included, before any target awareness.
- **Decoys.** The Season 04 2023 update describes "Hallucinations", cloned decoy players visible only to flagged accounts, and "Cloaking" ([Call of Duty blog](https://www.callofduty.com/blog/2023/06/call-of-duty-ricochet-anti-cheat-season-04-update)). An AI replay tool that scores a clip's "% chance of cheating" before human review was previewed in 2025 ([CharlieIntel on X](https://x.com/charlieINTEL/status/1920161710583009715)).

### C.2 Respawn and EA, Apex Legends

2026-03-04: using Strikepack, Cronus, XIM or Titan "to automate actions, modify recoil, alter input behavior, or simulate unintended control schemes to gain an unfair advantage is cheating. Full stop," with permanent bans and no appeals; the system "is being built to differentiate between legitimate accessibility tools and hardware used to cheat". No process is described ([EA](https://www.ea.com/games/apex-legends/apex-legends/news/breach-anti-cheat-update)). An EA forum thread titled "Input Spoofing via Virtual Gamepad Driver (ViGEmBus)" shows the community treats mouse-to-virtual-pad as an aim-assist exploit ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/idea/apex-legends-bug-reports-en/input-spoofing-via-virtual-gamepad-driver-vigembus/13176788)) **[title only; body and any staff reply not retrievable]**.

### C.3 Bungie, Destiny 2

TWAB 2023-04-13, still the policy: "External accessibility aides" are "any device or input that augments the player's ability to control the game beyond what the game itself normally provides. This includes ... programmable controllers, keyboard and mouse adapters, advanced macros, or automation via artificial intelligence." Bungie "embraces" aids "that enable an experience the game designers intended" and bans use "to mitigate challenges all players face, such as reduce recoil or increase aim assist"; PvE is evaluated too ([Bungie](https://www.bungie.net/7/en/News/article/twab-04-13-2023-best-dressed), [quoted thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1085660/discussions/0/5738190785824669858/)). This is the clearest published line, it names AI automation, and it does not exempt PvE.

### C.4 Ubisoft, Rainbow Six Siege

MouseTrap detects input spoofing on console and is "designed to reduce the chances of disabled players who use adaptive technology ... from being penalized"; "if you are a disabled player and feel unfairly penalized, there will be an in-game QR code that you can use to contact us" ([Ubisoft](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/rainbow-six/siege/news-updates/65UBprZeK2lHJw1qKI8ygM/mouse-and-keyboard-anticheat-feature-on-consoles)). The only publisher with a stated accessibility appeal channel.

### C.5 Epic, Fortnite and Easy Anti-Cheat

Cronus and Strike Pack users get a warning then a permanent ban; "hardware that provides, or is intended to provide, a competitive advantage is not allowed" while "accessibility devices, such as the Xbox Accessibility Controller and the 3dRudder are still totally fine" ([Fortnite Tracker](https://tracker.gg/fortnite/articles/you-can-now-be-banned-for-using-a-cronus-zen-in-fortnite)).

### C.6 Riot, Vanguard

No accessibility policy found. The documented case is the 2022 refusal to allow a disabled player's touchpad ("not optimal for competitive shooter games"), criticised by AbleGamers' Steven Spohn and never reversed publicly ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/valorant-accessibility-riot-games-touchpad-controller/)). Vanguard blocks drivers it considers vulnerable or unsigned ([VAN: Incompatible Software](https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/valorant/performance/van-incompatible-software)); Vanguard On-Demand (2026-06-24) requires Windows 11 25H2, Secure Boot, TPM 2.0 and VBS/HVCI ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/vanguard-on-demand)). Riot's anti-cheat director, May 2025, on outline colours: "Especially in Valorant with those bright outlines, you can almost do it with just an algorithm," and on premium aimbots: "You have to humanize the cheat to a degree where the advantage is imperceptible from what a human can do" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/03/how-riot-games-is-fighting-the-war-against-video-game-hackers/)).

### C.7 Others

- **nProtect GameGuard, Helldivers 2, April 2024.** Blocked Spohn's infrared head controller and, per tetraplegic players, AltController, VoiceAttack, reWASD and AutoHotkey; Arrowhead's CEO engaged personally, with no policy outcome ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/helldivers-2-anti-cheat-gamers-with-disabilities/), [FandomWire](https://fandomwire.com/helldivers-2-preventing-disabilities-arrowhead-ceo/)).
- **BattlEye** states it bans no one for "non-hack programs (like Fraps, overlays, etc.)" but blocks drivers with known exploitable flaws ([BattlEye support](https://www.battleye.com/support/)).
- **Virtual gamepads.** ViGEmBus was retired in November 2023 and still functions ([GitHub](https://github.com/nefarius/ViGEmBus)); neither it nor vJoy has a published ban or whitelist status from any vendor. DS4Windows runs under Vanguard using HidHide and FakerInput, which indicates the drivers themselves are not blocked ([DS4Windows docs](https://kanuan.github.io/DS4WSite/about/companion-drivers/)).

### C.8 Is screen capture or a detector process detected?

No vendor publicly claims to detect DXGI duplication, Windows.Graphics.Capture or an ONNX or YOLO process. The academic direction is the opposite:

- **AimTrap** (ACM CCS 2026, submitted June 2026): visual aimbots operate "on rendered game frames rather than accessing game memory"; "post-hoc behavior detectors ... are increasingly fragile against human-mimicking aimbots"; proposes adversarial camouflage textures (85.1 percent evasion of the aimbot's detector) and honeypot textures that bait the aimbot (96.9 percent detection of it) ([arXiv 2606.25734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25734)).
- **Shaikh, Ni and Dacier** (June 2026): memory-scanning anti-cheat "become[s] useless" against visual aimbots; proposes adversarial-patch honeytokens drawn into the HUD ([arXiv 2606.07650](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07650v1)).
- **Babin and Katchabaw** (ICEC 2023) is the earlier treatment ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1007/978-981-99-8248-6_24)).

Implication: the detection surface for a computer-vision assist is behavioural (input statistics) plus bait (decoys and honeytokens). A tool that engages a decoy generates its own evidence, and nothing on the tool's side can prevent that in a game that draws them.

Sources: [dxcam](https://github.com/ra1nty/DXcam), [dxcam on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/dxcam/) (0.0.5 in September 2022, then 0.1.0, 0.2.0, 0.3.0 in March 2026 and 0.4.0.dev1 on 2026-03-18), [RootKit BetterCam](https://github.com/RootKit-Org/BetterCam), [ZCban BetterCam](https://github.com/ZCban/BetterCam), [windows-capture](https://github.com/NiiightmareXD/windows-capture), [windows-capture on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/windows-capture), [python-mss](https://github.com/BoboTiG/python-mss), [mss `gdi.py`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BoboTiG/python-mss/main/src/mss/windows/gdi.py), [PIL against mss timing](https://www.pythontutorials.net/blog/fastest-way-to-take-a-screenshot-with-python-on-windows/).

**DXGI Desktop Duplication.** `AcquireNextFrame` returns a `B8G8R8A8` GPU surface of the whole output with dirty and move rectangles, or `DXGI_ERROR_WAIT_TIMEOUT` when no new frame arrived within the timeout. It is paced by the compositor: at 60 Hz there is one new frame every 16.7 ms, and spinning faster re-reads a stale frame (dxcam's README says the "1000+ fps" figures seen elsewhere are exactly that) ([Microsoft DDA overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/direct3ddxgi/desktop-dup-api)). Classic exclusive-fullscreen DX9 and OpenGL apps mostly cannot be captured; DirectX 11.1 and later exclusive apps can unless they opt out, and on Windows 10 and 11 nearly all games run through the flip model with fullscreen optimisations, which is why dxcam advertises stable capture of fullscreen Direct3D ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/desktop-duplication-api-is-unable-to-capture-desktop-frames-with-windows-10/228832)); DX12 is composed the same way **[not separately verified]**. Occlusion: the screen is what is captured, so an occluded game still appears, and so does everything on top of it (Nimbus's own always-on-top window, a Discord popup). Error rules from [DuplicateOutput](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/dxgi1_2/nf-dxgi1_2-idxgioutput1-duplicateoutput): `E_ACCESSDENIED` on the secure desktop (UAC prompt, lock screen), `DXGI_ERROR_UNSUPPORTED` for 8 bpp or non-DWM modes, `DXGI_ERROR_SESSION_DISCONNECTED` for a disconnected session (RDP), `DXGI_ERROR_NOT_CURRENTLY_AVAILABLE` when more than four processes duplicate at once, `DXGI_ERROR_ACCESS_LOST` on any desktop switch, mode change or fullscreen transition, after which the duplication object must be recreated. A session 0 service cannot use it. On Microsoft Hybrid laptops the D3D device must be created on the adapter that owns the output or DDA fails ([KB3019314](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/troubleshoot/windows-client/shell-experience/error-when-dda-capable-app-is-against-gpu)); dxcam exposes `device_idx` and `output_idx`. Every Python library stages the surface to CPU memory; ZCban's benchmark (RTX 4070, 5800X, 1080p) reads cupy 283 fps, torch 298, plain CPU 286, which says the readback and not colour conversion is the cost. Whether dxcam's `region=` copies a sub-rectangle or crops after a full staging copy is not documented **[unverified; in the 0.0.5 source the full texture was staged then cropped]**. Nobody publishes a cropped-region fps; the ceiling is the refresh rate regardless.

**Windows.Graphics.Capture.** Frames arrive as `Direct3D11CaptureFrame` objects from a `Direct3D11CaptureFramePool` with a QPC `SystemRelativeTime`; `FrameArrived` fires per frame; create the pool free-threaded so frames do not queue behind a UI thread ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5629458/windows-graphics-capture-delay-on-source-window-mo), [screen capture doc](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/media-authoring-processing/screen-capture)). Windows 10 1903 and later; per-window capture without the picker through `IGraphicsCaptureItemInterop::CreateForWindow` ([interop](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/windows.graphics.capture.interop/nf-windows-graphics-capture-interop-igraphicscaptureiteminterop-createforwindow)). Window capture reads the window's own composition surface, so "the target window renders even when occluded"; minimised windows are unreliable ([OBS window capture guide](https://obs-versions.com/blog/obs-window-capture-guide)). That is the property that excludes Nimbus's overlay from the frame. Behaviour under exclusive fullscreen and DX12 **[unverified]**. The system draws a yellow border; `IsBorderRequired = false` needs `GraphicsCaptureAccess.RequestAccessAsync(Borderless)` plus a manifest capability, is Windows 11 only, and is overridden if any other app still requires it ([IsBorderRequired](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/uwp/api/windows.graphics.capture.graphicscapturesession.isborderrequired)); windows-capture exposes `draw_border` and `cursor_capture`. Same secure-desktop and session rules as DDA.

**GDI.** mss's Windows backend is `GetWindowDC`, `CreateCompatibleDC`, `CreateDIBSection`, `BitBlt(SRCCOPY | CAPTUREBLT)`, `GdiFlush`, reading the DIB memory directly and caching it; CPU-side and DPI-aware ([gdi.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BoboTiG/python-mss/main/src/mss/windows/gdi.py)). The claim on some blogs that mss uses DXGI is wrong. GDI copies the composed desktop, so occlusion behaves like DDA, and cost scales with region, which makes a 320 px centre crop cheap, of the order of a millisecond. It cannot see a true exclusive-fullscreen surface that bypasses DWM, which is rare today.

**Consequence for Nimbus.** dxcam 0.3.0 wheels are 3.10 and up, windows-capture is 3.9 and up, mss 10 is 3.9 and up; only mss 9.x or PIL keep the documented 3.8 floor. CI already runs 3.11.

### D.4 Lighter alternatives to a trained model

**Colour keying.** Valorant lets players choose the enemy outline colour; Riot's anti-cheat director's "just an algorithm" remark (C.6) is about this. Public colour bots ([slyautomation](https://github.com/slyautomation/valorant_aimbot), [Xyrea guide](https://github.com/Xyrea/Valorant-Color-Aimbot-Guide), [CVM-colorBot](https://github.com/asenyeroao-ct/CVM-colorBot), [2PC-Valorant-Colorbot](https://github.com/Violevo/2PC-Valorant-Colorbot)) all do the same thing: an HSV or RGB threshold on the centre crop, morphology, contours, the nearest blob, aim at its top with an offset. Cost is well under a millisecond on a 320 px crop on CPU **[estimate]**. Fragility: the outline colour varies with distance (the Xyrea guide says so explicitly), HUD and kill-feed elements share the colour, and many games draw no outline. Overwatch and Fortnite have similar options; each game's setting name was not verified **[unverified]**.

**Left 4 Dead 2 outlines** (checked directly): the client cvars `cl_glow_survivor_r`, `_g`, `_b` set the survivor teammate glow colour, with `cl_glow_infected_*` and ghost-infected variants for the infected glows; values 0 to 1 per channel, all zero means no glow; they are not cheat-flagged and are commonly set in `autoexec.cfg` ([Steam guide, Change Outline Glows](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=942284942), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/550/discussions/0/43099721682403869/)). Which infected states glow for a survivor player in campaign was not verified and has to be measured on saved frames **[unverified]**.

**Template matching** suits fixed-appearance HUD elements (hit markers, reticle states), not characters at varying scale and pose. **Motion detection** (frame differencing, Farneback dense flow; [LearnOpenCV](https://learnopencv.com/moving-object-detection-with-opencv/)) is cheap but a rate-controlled stick moves the whole frame, so ego-motion has to be cancelled first (phase correlation, which `tests/frame_motion.py` already does for verdicts), and it cannot tell enemy from ally from scenery. **No accessibility tool using any of these was found**; both Aimmy and FNBUBBLES420's tool use YOLO.

### D.5 Game-provided data paths

For single-player titles a data path beats capture on every axis: exact 3D positions, no capture or inference latency, no false positives, no anti-cheat exposure. Its cost is per-game work, and it does not exist for multiplayer or anti-cheat-protected games, which is exactly where the CV tools operate.

- **Source engine.** `getpos`, `cl_showpos`, `cl_showents` and `ent_text` dump positions and the entity list behind `sv_cheats 1` ([Steam console guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2898420585)). The harness already drives Left 4 Dead 2 and Half-Life 2 through the console. No console command prints an entity's screen position; a SourceMod plugin could.
- **Arma 3 scripting** (checked directly). SQF has `worldToScreen`, and the standard pattern in player-tag scripts is `_unit modelToWorld (_unit selectionPosition "head_axis")` inside `onEachFrame`, then `worldToScreen`, then `drawIcon3D` ([fn_playerTags.sqf](https://github.com/HesienBurger/ArmA-3-Life/blob/master/ArmA3Life.LakesideValley/core/functions/fn_playerTags.sqf), [Bohemia forum on onEachFrame](https://forums.bohemia.net/forums/topic/167963-oneachframe-and-finddisplay-script-question/)). A known caveat: `modelToWorld` with `worldToScreen` in a moving vehicle gives a wrong position because of velocity error ([Bohemia forum](https://forums.bohemia.net/forums/topic/193816-modeltoworld-used-with-worldtoscreen-bad-position-given/)). `boundingBoxReal` gives box corners for a label. The Bohemia wiki itself returned HTTP 403 to the fetch, so the exact return value for an off-screen position was not read **[unverified; the forum examples handle it as an empty or out-of-range result]**.
- **Unreal.** UE4SS gives Lua and C++ access to live objects including the player pawn and `K2_GetActorLocation` ([UE4SS docs](https://docs.ue4ss.com/guides/creating-a-lua-mod.html)).
- **Unity.** BepInEx plugins; a Ravenfield single-player aim assist mod with ESP and exposed-body targeting exists ([Nexus](https://www.nexusmods.com/ravenfield/mods/15)) **[page not fetched]**.
- **Bethesda.** Skyrim Access, an SKSE plugin for blind players with NVDA speech, an object scanner, bow auto-aim and enemy lock-on ([Nexus](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/181131)) **[page not fetched]**. The strongest precedent for auto-aim as a recognised accessibility feature.

### D.6 Real-time constraints

**Where the milliseconds go.** No project publishes a measured capture-to-input figure for a same-PC CV aimbot **[gap]**. Assembled from components:

| Stage | Cost | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Frame age at capture | 8 to 17 ms at 60 Hz (half a refresh on average, up to one) | DDA and WGC deliver a frame only when the compositor produces one; the game finished rendering it a frame earlier |
| Readback and preprocessing | 1 to 4 ms for a 1080p staging copy, less for a crop | the 240 to 290 fps figures in D.1 |
| Inference | 1.5 to 5 ms on a mid-range GPU with a nano or small model; 15 to 70 ms on CPU by input size | D.2 |
| Output | sub-millisecond for `SendInput`; a ViGEm stick update is read at the game's next XInput poll, typically once per game frame, up to 16.7 ms at 60 fps | |
| Game, render, display | NVIDIA's Reflex chain measured 56 ms system latency at 60 Hz on a GTX 1660 Super in Fortnite without Reflex, 38 ms with | [NVIDIA Reflex](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/reflex-low-latency-platform/) |

So a loop observes the effect of its own command roughly 60 to 120 ms later. The visual loop cannot get new information faster than the refresh whatever its own rate: detection at 240 Hz on a 60 Hz game is wasted, but holding or interpolating the output between frames is not.

**Loop rates in the wild.** sunone defaults to 60 captures a second; RootKit reports 100 to 150 iterations a second on an RTX 3080; dxcam's benchmark targets 240; the 2026 arXiv aimbot ran at 15 fps; Lunar was bottlenecked by `SendInput`. Cheat sellers advertise sub-3 ms "capture to coordinates" with TensorRT, which excludes the frame age above.

**Rate control against position control.** Every public CV project drives a mouse delta or a hardware mouse (Arduino, KMBox, MAKCU). No public CV aimbot that drives a virtual gamepad stick was found **[searched; none found]**. A mouse is a position increment: `counts = degrees * dpi / (360 * sens)` in sunone's formula. A stick is an angular-velocity command. JoyShockMapper expresses aim-stick sensitivity as degrees per second at full tilt (`STICK_SENS`, default 360) with a response exponent (`STICK_POWER`) and an optional ramp (`STICK_ACCELERATION_RATE`), and calibrates the game with `REAL_WORLD_CALIBRATION` by performing a full 360 degree turn and running `CALCULATE_REAL_WORLD_CALIBRATION` ([JoyShockMapper README](https://github.com/JibbSmart/JoyShockMapper/blob/master/README.md), [GyroWiki](http://gyrowiki.jibbsmart.com/blog:joyshockmapper-guide)). XIM Matrix does the mirror-image calibration for mouse-to-stick: reticle on a landmark, one full horizontal turn, then floor to ceiling vertically, repeated for ADS, yielding reticle velocity per input ([XIM velocity calibration](https://guide.xim.tech/Velocity-Calibration/)). Nimbus's harness calibration (degrees per second per magnitude, per game; part H.2) is the same measurement.

Consequences for a stick-driven control law: the game's deadzone and inner curve must be inverted; the command saturates at the game's maximum turn rate; the controller is a velocity servo on an angular error rather than a one-shot displacement; and with 60 to 120 ms of dead time a proportional gain that feels snappy oscillates. For an integrating plant with pure delay τ the proportional loop is stable for `Kp * τ` below π/2 and comfortable below a third of that, so at τ = 0.1 s a `Kp` of 3 to 5 per second. Hold the last command between frames, keep the gain low, lead the target rather than chase it, and do not use an integral term against a target that steps away.

## E. Academic and HCI work

### E.1 Desktop target-aware pointing

- **Sticky icons and area cursors**: Worden et al., CHI 1997 ([ACM 10.1145/258549.258724](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/258549.258724)) **[DOI from memory, not fetched]**.
- **Semantic pointing** enlarges targets in motor space only: Blanch et al., CHI 2004 ([ACM 10.1145/985692.985758](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/985692.985758)) **[DOI from memory, not fetched]**.
- **The bubble cursor** resizes its activation area so exactly one target is always capturable: mean movement time 0.879 s against 1.250 s for the point cursor in the 1D study (about 30 percent faster, significant in all 27 conditions); 0.93 s against 1.08 s (object pointing) and 1.41 s (point cursor) in dense 2D layouts; error rates 2.35 against 3.16 percent and 1.58 against 2.98 percent ([Grossman and Balakrishnan, CHI 2005](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/papers/tgrossman_CHI2005.pdf)).
- **For motor-impaired users**, Findlater et al.'s enhanced area cursors (Visual-Motor-Magnifier, Click-and-Cross) cut small-target selection time by 19 percent, corrective submovements by 45 percent and error rate by up to 82 percent against the point cursor, and were unanimously preferred over the bubble cursor ([UIST 2010](https://faculty.washington.edu/wobbrock/pubs/uist-10.pdf)). Gajos's area pointing against goal crossing is the other standard reference ([ASSETS 2007](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/assets-07.pdf)).

### E.2 Games aim-assist taxonomy and effect sizes

- **Taxonomy** (Schneider and Graham, CHI 2023; Refai, Bateman and Fleming, 2020): Area Cursor, Bullet or Projectile Magnetism, Sticky Targets (slow the cursor once on target), Target Gravity (steer toward targets), Target Lock (snap and hold; "rare in shooter games" because of its power), plus slowdown and auto-aim; Refai et al. give a 27-technique framework across pointing, aiming, visual search, reaction time and steering ([Schneider PDF](https://equis.cs.queensu.ca/~equis/pubs/2023/schneider-chi-2023.pdf), [Refai, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2020.00017/full)). Schneider's contribution is a rapidly trainable per-player spatiotemporal aiming model (72 participants) meant to set assist strength from observed ability, the principled way to size an accessibility assist.
- **Bateman et al., CHI 2011**: area cursor and target gravity "significantly reduced score differential" between skilled and less-skilled players in a shooting gallery; unassisted players did not perform worse but noticed the relative change; less-skilled players "reported having more fun" and still approved after being told ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1978942.1979287)).
- **Vicencio-Moreira, Mandryk, Gutwin and Bateman, CHI 2014**, three studies of five assists in an Unreal-based FPS: assists "worked well in a target-range scenario" but "performance was reduced when game elements were introduced"; bullet magnetism and area cursor "worked well in a wide variety of situations"; target gravity improved headshots; sticky targets "had no effect on performance but was perceived"; "other techniques that worked well were too perceptible" ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2556288.2557308)) **[exact percentages paywalled; qualitative findings only]**. Their CIG 2014 follow-up used the same assists to balance multiplayer matches ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283130814_Balancing_multiplayer_first-person_shooter_games_using_aiming_assistance)).
- **Gutwin et al., CHI PLAY 2016, "Does Helping Hurt?"**: assistance "did significantly improve performance and perceived competence when it was present" with "no significant differences in performance gains" between assisted and unassisted groups, so assistance does not impede learning ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/2967934.2968101)).

Recurring design takeaways: magnetism and area-cursor help is the most effective and least noticed; sticky and lock techniques are the most noticeable and read as cheating to opponents; efficacy drops in real maps with occlusion and movement.

### E.3 Gaze aiming

Isokoski et al. (UAIS 2009) conclude eye trackers "are not competitive with the sub-pixel positioning accuracy of modern mice", that "acquiring and following targets with eye-based aiming is easy" but small targets are hard, and that "aiming assistance ... could be used when eye tracker FPS performance needs to be competitive with players using a mouse. In one player situations such systems may not be necessary" ([PDF](https://homepages.tuni.fi/oleg.spakov/publications/Isokoski_UAIS_09.pdf)); their 2006 FPS study found gaze plus keyboard beat a gamepad but not a mouse ([Tampere](https://researchportal.tuni.fi/en/publications/eye-tracker-input-in-first-person-shooter-games/)). A 2020 gaze-control accessibility study is paywalled ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-58796-3_25)).

### E.4 Shared control and software copilots

Ahmetovic, Manzoni, Corti and Mascetti: 14 interviews on human shared control for disabled players (CHI 2026; participants were open to software automation replacing the human copilot) and GamePals, a framework for both human and software copilots in unmodified third-party games, evaluated with 13 upper-limb-impaired players (January 2026) ([arXiv 2509.02132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02132), [arXiv 2601.11218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11218)). This is the academic frame that fits Nimbus: partial automation as a software copilot.

- **Activision.** The SEP lists aimbots and "input mapping software" and "any other software used to deliberately modify game data"; the software licence is at [activision.com](https://www.activision.com/legal/software-terms-of-use).
- **EA.** An "Unauthorized Third-Party Program" is anything EA believes "enables or facilitates cheating", bypasses or modifies the game interface, or "intercepts or collects information from the game" ([EA User Agreement](https://www.ea.com/legal/user-agreement)). Screen capture plausibly "collects information".
- **Riot.** Prohibits programs that "interact with the Riot Services in any way" and any that "collect info ... by reading areas of memory" ([Riot ToS](https://www.riotgames.com/en/terms-of-service)).
- **CVAA** (as amended for games, 2019): covers in-game communication and the UI to reach it, "not the actual gameplay" ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/accessibility-communications-video-games), [IGDA GASIG](https://igda-gasig.org/what-and-why/about-cvaa/)).
- **ADA Title III.** No court has held it applies to gameplay; the 2024 DOJ WCAG rule is Title II (government) only; litigation is about storefronts and websites ([ABA, August 2025](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2025-august/digital-accessibility-under-title-iii-ada/), [LMU Law Review](https://digitalcommons.lmu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3214&context=llr)).
- **EU Accessibility Act** (in force 2025-06-28; 2030 transition for pre-existing services): applies to in-game chat, e-commerce, consoles, launchers and web and mobile games; "gameplay itself is not covered"; its operability rules (sequential control, alternatives to fine motor control, no simultaneous inputs) apply at the product and UI layer; GPSR (December 2024) adds safety duties ([IGDA GASIG](https://igda-gasig.org/what-and-why/demystifying-eaa-gpsr/), [Player Research](https://www.playerresearch.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-video-games-going-over-the-facts-june-2025/), [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/03/accessibility-in-the-gaming-industry)).

No law obliges a publisher to accept third-party input assistance, and none forbids a user running one on a single-player game.
